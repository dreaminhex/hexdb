HexDB 2.5.4 fixes a way a sliced lattice could lose documents. Upgrade every lattice that uses `replication.slicing`.

## Fixed

- **Data loss after a full sync cut short (slicing).** A replica copying everything from the Overseer rewrites its data one tessellation at a time, dropping the slices it no longer keeps, and only recorded what it held at the end. If a restart or crash cut that copy short, the hex still claimed every slice it held before: other replicas counted it as a copy, and once elected Overseer it served what was left as everything, and the replicas copied it. Now a full sync first records that a copy is in progress and that it holds no slice. Such a hex is never counted as a copy, is never elected Overseer (not even alone), and copies everything again on its next start.
- **Leftovers in collected slices.** A new Overseer clears what it has in a slice before collecting it from a replica, so leftovers of an interrupted copy can't bring back deleted documents.
- **Startup while collecting slices.** An Overseer collecting slices no longer exits at startup because it can't write its security defaults yet; it sets them up once it can.

## Notes

- The 2.5.1 release has no macOS disk image (its build ran out of space); 2.5.2 and later have one.

## Upgrading

Restart the hexes one at a time, as usual: replicas first, the Overseer last. To check a sliced lattice's data, compare each tessellation's count on the Overseer with what its replicas hold (each hex's `/status`, `metrics.tessellations`).
